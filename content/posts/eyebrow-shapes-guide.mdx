Choosing between eyebrow shapes is easier when you can compare them on the same face. Start with the brows you already have, choose one change to explore, and check both the close-up and the full portrait. A style name gives you a direction; it does not decide what you have to like.

This guide uses five common shape descriptions. The examples describe the contour, not a promise that every face will look the same with that shape.

## Five eyebrow shapes to compare

| Shape       | Contour to look for                                         | A useful comparison                                                  |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Natural     | A contour close to your existing brow placement             | Use it as a baseline before trying a stronger change                 |
| Arched      | A more noticeable rise toward the brow's high point         | Compare the arch height with your original                           |
| Straight    | A flatter direction from the inner brow toward the tail     | Compare how much of the original rise you want to keep               |
| Soft Angled | A gentle change in direction around the arch                | Compare it with Natural and Arched on the same photo                 |
| S-Shaped    | A curved contour with a change in curvature before the arch | Inspect the whole contour rather than only raising its highest point |

S-Shaped is included here as a shape description. It is currently unavailable in the Browlens filter because the existing engine has no matching preset. The tool identifies available options before you choose.

## Shape is more than arch height

Two brows can have a similar arch and still look different. Length changes where the tail ends. Thickness changes the visual weight of the contour. Angle changes its direction, while spacing and overall height change its placement.

The [Browlens eyebrow editor](/#brow-app) lets you explore these details separately. You can drag six points across the two brows, adjust thickness, length, angle, spacing and height, and compare arch-height and brow-length differences in a report. Work on one detail at a time so you can tell which change you prefer.

## Choose an eyebrow shape by face shape

Face-shape advice is a classic starting point, not a rule about what you should wear. Judge your own face shape by looking at the overall length, width, forehead, cheekbones, and jaw in a relaxed, front-facing photo. Browlens does not detect or classify face shape; it lets you compare brow outlines on your own photo.

Use the [eyebrow shape for face shape guide](/eyebrow-shape-for-face-shape) to choose a first comparison:

- **Round:** Start with Soft Angled or Arched. Compare a gentle rise with a more noticeable high point, while keeping the tail and thickness similar. You are testing how much definition you enjoy, not trying to erase roundness. See [eyebrow shapes for round faces](/blog/eyebrow-shapes-for-round-faces).
- **Oval:** Compare Natural with Soft Angled. Begin close to your existing contour, then check whether a small change in direction adds something you like. There is no need to choose a dramatic arch. Read [eyebrow shapes for oval faces](/blog/eyebrow-shapes-for-oval-faces).
- **Square:** Try a soft Arched contour or Natural. If you explore Arched, keep the rise gentle rather than assuming a pronounced peak is necessary. Look at the brow alongside your jawline in the full portrait. Explore [eyebrow shapes for square faces](/blog/eyebrow-shapes-for-square-faces).
- **Heart:** Start with Natural or Soft Angled with a lower arch. Compare a modest rise before increasing the height, and check how the brow sits within the forehead area. Our guide to [eyebrow shapes for heart-shaped faces](/blog/eyebrow-shapes-for-heart-shaped-faces) develops that comparison.
- **Long:** Begin with Straight. Compare a flatter direction with your original rise, without assuming that every curve needs to disappear. Pay attention to the whole brow rather than only its highest point. See [eyebrow shapes for long faces](/blog/eyebrow-shapes-for-long-faces).
- **Diamond:** Try Soft Angled as a starting point. Look for a gentle transition around the arch, then judge it alongside the width of your cheekbones rather than in an isolated brow crop.

If two face-shape descriptions seem plausible, compare the suggested outlines from both. You do not need a perfect label to make a useful choice. Keep Natural as a reference, and use the free eyebrow filter to compare it with one alternative at a time.

## How to compare without getting lost in small changes

Use one photo for the whole comparison. Keep the same expression and framing rather than judging one style on a close-up and another on a different portrait. Check where the brow starts, how its direction changes, and where it ends.

Save the result you prefer and compare it again with your starting photo. A generated image is visual inspiration; it cannot guarantee how a later styling choice will look.

## Common mistakes when comparing eyebrow shapes

A comparison works best when you can explain what changed. Watch for these easy distractions:

- **Changing several features together.** A fuller, longer, higher brow may look different for several reasons. In the editor, keep the other controls steady while you test one change. Otherwise, you might credit the arch for a difference caused by thickness.
- **Accepting misplaced starting points.** Before judging a contour, check that its head and tail sit where you intend. A shape can seem wrong simply because its placement needs correcting. Use the full editor when you want to adjust those points manually.
- **Judging only at high zoom.** Enlarge the image to inspect the outline, then return to the full portrait. Small differences that dominate a crop may not matter to your overall impression. Do not force both sides into an identical contour just to satisfy the close-up.
- **Treating a preview as a removal plan.** An outline shows an idea, not which hairs to remove. Compare it with your actual hair coverage and try a reversible makeup version before considering a physical change.

If two options still feel equally good, note the one detail you prefer in each. You may want the familiar placement of Natural with only a slightly softer transition at the arch, rather than an entirely different shape.
