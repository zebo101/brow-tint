Learning how to shape eyebrows starts with deciding what you want to change. You may want a softer arch, a shorter tail, or a little more definition while keeping your familiar outline. Planning on a photo gives you a way to compare those choices before changing your actual brows.

This beginner's guide focuses on understanding placement and testing a look. It does not require you to remove hairs to complete any of the steps.

## 1. Start with the brows you have

Brush your brow hairs into their usual direction so you can see the outline, then take a clear, front-facing photo with both brows visible. Keep your expression relaxed and use even lighting.

For a simple makeup approach, [Benefit's natural eyebrow guide](https://www.benefitcosmetics.com/en-gb/articles/brows/how-to-fill-in-your-eyebrows-naturally.html) recommends working with your existing shape and filling sparse areas selectively. That is also a useful starting point for a digital comparison: preserve what you already like before adding changes.

## 2. Find the head, arch, and tail

Identify the inner start, the higher part of the brow, and the outer end on each side. Reference systems such as [Anastasia Beverly Hills' three-point method](https://www.anastasiabeverlyhills.com/pages/the-golden-ratio) use facial landmarks to help locate these areas.

You do not have to copy a diagram exactly. Compare the points with the hairs and proportions visible on your own photo. Our [eyebrow mapping guide](/blog/eyebrow-mapping-guide) explains what each point represents.

## Tailor the outline to your face shape

Use your face shape as a way to choose a comparison, not as a rule for changing your brows. Judge the overall proportions yourself in a relaxed, front-facing photo. Browlens does not detect or classify face shape, so a detected brow outline is not a face-shape recommendation.

The [eyebrow shape for face shape guide](/eyebrow-shape-for-face-shape) gives classic starting points. For example:

- For a round face, compare Soft Angled or Arched with your original. Test a gentle rise first and check whether you actually prefer more definition. See [eyebrow shapes for round faces](/blog/eyebrow-shapes-for-round-faces).
- For a square face, compare a soft Arched contour or Natural. Keep the transition around the arch gentle while you assess the full portrait. Our guide to [eyebrow shapes for square faces](/blog/eyebrow-shapes-for-square-faces) offers more context.

If your features do not fit one category neatly, you can still make a useful choice. Use the free eyebrow filter to compare Natural, Arched, Straight, and Soft Angled on the same photo. Choose one feature to preserve and one to test, such as keeping your familiar thickness while exploring a lower arch.

## 3. Correct the digital placement

Upload your photo to the [Browlens brow app](/#brow-app). AI detects facial and eyebrow landmarks and overlays guide lines for the brow heads, arches, and tails.

If a guide point needs correcting, use the six-point editor. Drag the head, arch, or tail on either side. This lets you correct the detected placement before choosing the look you want to preview.

## 4. Change one detail at a time

| Control     | What to compare                       |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Thickness   | A lighter or fuller contour           |
| Length      | Where the tail ends                   |
| Angle       | The direction of the brow             |
| Spacing     | The distance between the brows        |
| Height      | The overall vertical placement        |
| Arch height | The rise around the brow's high point |

Start with a small adjustment, look at the full portrait, and then decide whether to keep it. The editor can adjust both brows together or refine one side. Avoid changing every setting at once, which makes it difficult to see what caused the difference.

## 5. Read the report in context

Browlens reports arch-height and brow-length differences between the two sides. These are measurements from the photo in pixels, not a beauty score or a requirement that both brows match perfectly.

If a difference surprises you, check the photo angle and guide placement before making another adjustment. The report is most useful alongside the visible image.

## 6. Preview the finished idea

Confirm your placement, select a brow style, and generate an AI photo preview. Compare the original with the result before deciding which direction you prefer. Keep the downloaded image as inspiration, with the understanding that a preview is not a guarantee of a later physical result.

If you only want to compare preset shapes, use the [eyebrow filter and simulator](/filter). It handles detection automatically and skips manual positioning. Local outlines are free to explore; generating a new AI photo requires sign-in and credits.

## Maintain the result and know when to ask a professional

Keep your original photo alongside the preferred preview, and write down what you liked: the tail length, a gentler arch, or a fuller inner section. That gives you a reference more specific than a style name. If you try the idea with makeup, brush the hairs into their usual direction first and build definition gradually.

Review the brows without makeup before deciding that the outline needs changing again. Check at normal viewing distance as well as close up. Avoid repeatedly narrowing the brow or shortening the tail just because a few hairs sit differently on a particular day. There is no single maintenance schedule that suits every brow; reassess when the shape looks different to you.

Consider a brow professional when you want a substantial physical change, cannot tell which hairs form the outline, or feel unsure about matching a preview to your existing coverage. Bring the original and the preferred image. Explain what you want to keep, ask what is practical with your brows, and agree on the intended thickness, arch, and tail before any shaping begins.

## Troubleshoot a shape that does not feel right

Return to the original photo before adding more changes. Try a targeted check rather than starting over:

- **The arch looks too sharp:** Compare Natural or Soft Angled with the current idea. In the full editor, test a lower arch without changing the overall brow height at the same time.
- **The brows feel too heavy:** Check thickness before moving the whole outline. If testing with makeup, compare a lightly filled version with a more defined one rather than assuming the shape itself is wrong.
- **The tail looks too long or low:** Inspect its endpoint and direction against the original. Correct that area in the editor before changing spacing or moving the inner brow.
- **One side seems higher:** Check your expression, head angle, and guide points first. If the difference remains in a relaxed photo, decide whether it bothers you in the full portrait before trying to level it.
- **The preview extends beyond your brow hairs:** Treat it as an idea to discuss or test with temporary makeup, not evidence that removing hairs will produce it. A different outline may be easier to work with.
