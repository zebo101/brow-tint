When choosing eyebrows for heart shaped face proportions, start with Natural or Soft Angled and look for a lower, softer arch. A slightly fuller tail can keep the brow from fading abruptly after its highest point, without requiring a heavy outline or a dramatic extension.

These are comparison ideas, not rules about what your face should look like. A broader forehead and a narrower chin do not need correcting. Use the guidance to pick a starting shape, then let your own photo show whether you prefer a softer direction or your familiar brows.

## How to recognise a heart-shaped face

Stand in front of a mirror with your hair away from your forehead and jaw. A heart-shaped face often has a broader upper section, with width through the forehead or cheekbones, followed by a jaw that narrows toward the chin. The chin may look more pointed than the softer outline above it.

Check length as well as width. Your face may be longer than wide, but the noticeable taper from the upper face toward the lower face is the more useful clue. Do not use a pointed chin alone to decide; compare the forehead, cheekbones, and jaw together.

A widow's peak is not required and does not settle the category on its own. Use a level, front-facing photo if a side part or hairstyle makes the outline hard to judge. If the jaw narrows more gradually toward a rounded chin, compare the [oval face eyebrow guide](/blog/eyebrow-shapes-for-oval-faces). If length is the clearest feature, the [long face guide](/blog/eyebrow-shapes-for-long-faces) offers another starting point.

## Choose softness before extra height

Natural can keep your current brow direction while letting you assess fullness and the tail. Soft Angled offers a little more definition around the turn. With either, look for a low, gentle transition rather than a sharply raised peak.

If you prefer a visible curve, compare Arched as an alternative, but pay attention to how much height it adds. You do not need to select the most dramatic version of a shape. Our [eyebrow shape for face shape guide](/eyebrow-shape-for-face-shape) explains why these are starting suggestions rather than fixed matches.

| Shape       | Why it may suit this face                              | What to compare                                            |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Natural     | Keeps a familiar contour with room for a softer finish | Existing arch and tail fullness versus stronger definition |
| Soft Angled | Adds a gentle turn without needing a high peak         | A lower bend versus a more pronounced angle                |
| Arched      | Offers an alternative if you enjoy a visible curve     | A modest rise versus height that dominates the upper face  |

## Inspect the brow from start to tail

A lower arch is only part of the idea. The way the brow begins and finishes can change how soft the whole contour feels, even when the highest point stays similar.

**Start:** Keep the inner brow lightly defined and close to its usual placement. A dark, squared block may draw attention to the center of the forehead more than you intended. Compare small amounts of definition rather than making the start much heavier to match a fuller tail.

**Arch position:** Look for a gradual turn toward the outer portion of the brow. A peak near the middle can make the contour seem more pointed. Check the full portrait to judge whether a lower rise feels calmer to you, without assuming the arch must disappear entirely.

**Tail length:** Preserve a gentle outward taper that remains visible after the arch. Slightly fuller means a little more continuity through that taper, not a thick line carried far toward the temple. A longer endpoint and a fuller tail are separate choices, so do not confuse one with the other.

**Thickness:** Keep the body and tail connected in visual weight. A substantial inner section that suddenly becomes a very fine tail can exaggerate the difference between them. Start with your existing density and ask whether a small amount of tail definition is enough.

Use the [eyebrow mapping guide](/blog/eyebrow-mapping-guide) to understand these reference areas. Their placement should help you describe your own brows, not replace your features with a standard diagram.

## What to avoid when you want a gentler contour

Very high, pointed arches can add more emphasis to the upper face when your goal is softness. Compare a lower outline before deciding you need extra lift. Equally, a very short or barely visible tail may not give you the continuous shape you wanted.

Avoid solving a faint tail by darkening the entire brow. That can change the visual weight much more than a small, targeted adjustment. A soft start and a visible taper can coexist.

Do not compare photos with different head tilts or expressions. Raising your forehead in one image can make the arch appear higher and turn a small styling comparison into a comparison of expressions.

## Make a small, clear styling plan

Write down the single change you liked most: a softer turn, a more visible tail, or less emphasis at the start. Try that idea temporarily with makeup before considering any change to actual hairs.
