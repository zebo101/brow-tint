Choosing between eyebrow shapes is easier when you can compare them on the same face. Start with the brows you already have, choose one change to explore, and check both the close-up and the full portrait. A style name gives you a direction; it does not decide what you have to like.

This guide uses five common shape descriptions. The examples describe the contour, not a promise that every face will look the same with that shape.

## Five eyebrow shapes to compare

| Shape | Contour to look for | A useful comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural | A contour close to your existing brow placement | Use it as a baseline before trying a stronger change |
| Arched | A more noticeable rise toward the brow's high point | Compare the arch height with your original |
| Straight | A flatter direction from the inner brow toward the tail | Compare how much of the original rise you want to keep |
| Soft Angled | A gentle change in direction around the arch | Compare it with Natural and Arched on the same photo |
| S-Shaped | A curved contour with a change in curvature before the arch | Inspect the whole contour rather than only raising its highest point |

S-Shaped is included here as a shape description. It is currently unavailable in the Browlens filter because the existing engine has no matching preset. The tool identifies available options before you choose.

## Shape is more than arch height

Two brows can have a similar arch and still look different. Length changes where the tail ends. Thickness changes the visual weight of the contour. Angle changes its direction, while spacing and overall height change its placement.

The [Browlens eyebrow editor](/#brow-app) lets you explore these details separately. You can drag six points across the two brows, adjust thickness, length, angle, spacing and height, and compare arch-height and brow-length differences in a report. Work on one detail at a time so you can tell which change you prefer.

The instant outline is a planning preview. AI photo generation takes time and uses credits after sign-in. Keeping those stages separate lets you explore placement before generating an image.

## How to compare without getting lost in small changes

Use one photo for the whole comparison. Keep the same expression and framing rather than judging one style on a close-up and another on a different portrait. Check where the brow starts, how its direction changes, and where it ends.

Save the result you prefer and compare it again with your starting photo. A generated image is visual inspiration; it cannot guarantee how a later styling choice will look.
