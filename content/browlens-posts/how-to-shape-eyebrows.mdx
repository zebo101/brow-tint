Learning how to shape eyebrows starts with deciding what you want to change. You may want a softer arch, a shorter tail, or a little more definition while keeping your familiar outline. Planning on a photo gives you a way to compare those choices before changing your actual brows.

This beginner's guide focuses on understanding placement and testing a look. It does not require you to remove hairs to complete any of the steps.

## 1. Start with the brows you have

Brush your brow hairs into their usual direction so you can see the outline, then take a clear, front-facing photo with both brows visible. Keep your expression relaxed and use even lighting.

For a simple makeup approach, [Benefit's natural eyebrow guide](https://www.benefitcosmetics.com/en-gb/articles/brows/how-to-fill-in-your-eyebrows-naturally.html) recommends working with your existing shape and filling sparse areas selectively. That is also a useful starting point for a digital comparison: preserve what you already like before adding changes.

## 2. Find the head, arch, and tail

Identify the inner start, the higher part of the brow, and the outer end on each side. Reference systems such as [Anastasia Beverly Hills' three-point method](https://www.anastasiabeverlyhills.com/pages/the-golden-ratio) use facial landmarks to help locate these areas.

You do not have to copy a diagram exactly. Compare the points with the hairs and proportions visible on your own photo. Our [eyebrow mapping guide](/blog/eyebrow-mapping-guide) explains what each point represents.

## 3. Correct the digital placement

Upload your photo to the [Browlens brow app](/#brow-app). AI detects facial and eyebrow landmarks and overlays guide lines for the brow heads, arches, and tails.

If a guide point needs correcting, use the six-point editor. Drag the head, arch, or tail on either side. This lets you correct the detected placement before choosing the look you want to preview.

## 4. Change one detail at a time

| Control | What to compare |
| --- | --- |
| Thickness | A lighter or fuller contour |
| Length | Where the tail ends |
| Angle | The direction of the brow |
| Spacing | The distance between the brows |
| Height | The overall vertical placement |
| Arch height | The rise around the brow's high point |

Start with a small adjustment, look at the full portrait, and then decide whether to keep it. The editor can adjust both brows together or refine one side. Avoid changing every setting at once, which makes it difficult to see what caused the difference.

## 5. Read the report in context

Browlens reports arch-height and brow-length differences between the two sides. These are measurements from the photo in pixels, not a beauty score or a requirement that both brows match perfectly.

If a difference surprises you, check the photo angle and guide placement before making another adjustment. The report is most useful alongside the visible image.

## 6. Preview the finished idea

Confirm your placement, select a brow style, and generate an AI photo preview. Compare the original with the result before deciding which direction you prefer. Keep the downloaded image as inspiration, with the understanding that a preview is not a guarantee of a later physical result.

If you only want to compare preset shapes, use the [eyebrow filter and simulator](/filter). It handles detection automatically and skips manual positioning. Local outlines are free to explore; generating a new AI photo requires sign-in and credits.
